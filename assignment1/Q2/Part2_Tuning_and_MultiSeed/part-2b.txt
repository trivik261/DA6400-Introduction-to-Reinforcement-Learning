--- Comparison ---
  Q-Learning (off-policy) updates towards the max Q-value of the next
  state, directly estimating the optimal action-value function. This
  usually leads to faster convergence and higher final returns.

  SARSA (on-policy) updates towards the Q-value of the action actually
  taken under ε-greedy, so it learns a policy that accounts for ongoing
  exploration. This makes it more conservative but also more stable in
  environments where exploratory actions can be costly.

  As ε decays towards 0, both algorithms converge towards the same
  optimal Q-values, but Q-Learning typically gets there faster because
  its target is always the greedy (max) value.
