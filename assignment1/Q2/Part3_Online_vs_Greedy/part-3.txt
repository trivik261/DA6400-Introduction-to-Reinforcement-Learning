--- Explanation ---
  Online vs Greedy gap:
    The greedy policy (ε=0) outperforms the online performance (ε=0.1)
    because 10% random actions during training cause suboptimal steps,
    increasing episode length and accumulating more negative reward.

  SARSA vs Q-Learning — online:
    SARSA (on-policy) learns values under the ε-greedy behaviour policy
    it actually follows. Its online returns directly reflect the quality
    of its learned Q-values. With ε fixed at 0.1, the agent never fully
    exploits and online returns plateau.

  SARSA vs Q-Learning — greedy:
    Q-Learning (off-policy) always updates towards max_a Q(s',a),
    directly estimating the optimal (greedy) value function regardless
    of the exploratory actions taken. Therefore its greedy policy after
    training tends to be better than SARSA's, whose Q-values were shaped
    by the ε-greedy behaviour and are slightly conservative.

  Since ε never reaches 0 (fixed at 0.1), the persistent exploration
  creates a permanent gap between online and greedy performance. This
  contrasts with a decaying schedule (ε→0) where the gap eventually
  vanishes as the agent becomes fully greedy.
